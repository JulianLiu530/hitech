## SLIDE 1 — Cover (~15 sec)

> Good afternoon, Professor and classmates. We are Circuit Minds, Group 2. Today we present our strategic consulting project for HiTech Techno Limited — a Hong Kong-based B2B IT component distributor founded in 2018. Our theme is "From Word-of-Mouth to World-of-Mouth." Let's begin.

---

## SLIDE 2 — Diageo Way of Selling (~40 sec)

> Before we dive in, a quick roadmap. We've structured this deck using the Diageo Way of Selling — five steps. First, we summarize the Situation — the growth ceiling Ben faces. Second, we state the Big IDEA in one sentence. Third, we explain How It Works using Class 1 through 5 frameworks. Fourth, we reinforce Key Benefits with hard numbers. And fifth, we close with an Easy Next Step — a ¥2,000, 30-day, fully reversible pilot. Each step maps directly to a section of our deck. No filler.

---

## SLIDE 3 — Divider: Situation (~10 sec)

> Let's start with Step 1: Summarize the Situation. Where HiTech stands today, why we picked them, and the specific issue worth solving.

---

## SLIDE 4 — How We Picked HiTech (Q1) (~50 sec)

> We screened four candidate firms through three filters. First, real operations — HiTech processes 120 to 150 orders per month and has an 8-year track record. Second, verifiable assets — they hold official Tier-1 authorizations from NVIDIA and Samsung, which we confirmed with documentation. Third, stated expansion intent — founder Ben explicitly told us he wants to enter the mainland China market.
>
> Why does it fit this course? HiTech is a classic SME paradox: physically strong but digitally invisible. That tension made it the perfect canvas to apply VRIO, Five Forces, Value Chain, and Generic Strategy — all five classes of our syllabus.

---

## SLIDE 5 — The Ceiling Ben Hit (~40 sec)

> Here's the situation in three numbers. HiTech has 40 active accounts, 100% sourced through personal referrals, with zero digital presence anywhere on the mainland. Their quote response time is 48 hours because everything runs through manual PDFs on WhatsApp. And their impression count on mainland B2B search platforms? Zero.
>
> But here's the opportunity: mainland procurement managers are actively searching for Hong Kong Tier-1 authorized stock — especially for GPUs and NVMe drives where counterfeits are rampant. HiTech's offline assets are exactly what they want. They're just completely unreachable today.

---

## SLIDE 6 — Why This Issue (Q2) (~35 sec)

> Why focus on this issue and not something else? Three reasons. It's important — without digital reach, HiTech caps at 40 accounts forever and faces single-shock risk if any major client churns. It's urgent — the industry is in what Class 2 calls punctuated equilibrium. Digitization is happening now, not in five years. And it's solvable — this is not a multi-year, multi-million transformation. We can prove the concept with ¥5,000 and six months.
>
> The strategic issue: unlocking trapped competitive advantage through digital organization.

---

## SLIDE 7 — How We Analyzed It (Q3 & Q4) (~45 sec)

> Our analysis combined three methods. First-hand: we conducted three 45-minute depth interviews with Ben and spent a full day shadowing his customs clearance and order routing workflow. Archival: we reviewed Tier-1 authorization contracts, internal sales ledgers from 2022 to 2025, and ran a competitor SEO audit across 1688 and Baidu. Buyer audit: we interviewed 10 actual mainland procurement managers to map their real sourcing behavior.
>
> As a group, we ran weekly Saturday red-team sessions where each member took turns playing the grading professor — attacking every slide for logic, evidence, and grading criteria compliance.

---

## SLIDE 8 — Divider: Big IDEA (~10 sec)

> Step 2: State the Big IDEA. One sentence, in Ben's language, with numbers he can defend.

---

## SLIDE 9 — The Big IDEA (~30 sec)

> Our strategy is Focused Differentiation. The idea in one sentence: Turn 8 years of hidden trust into searchable, verifiable, 4-hour digital advantage.
>
> The parameters: ¥5,000 maximum capital at risk. 12-month timeline. Target outcome: 10 or more new mainland enterprise accounts. Projected ROI: 100 to 1. These are numbers Ben can repeat to his brother and to his bank.

---

## SLIDE 10 — Divider: How It Works (~10 sec)

> Step 3: Explain How It Works. We'll walk through our framework analysis first, then three concrete activity pillars.

---

## SLIDE 11 — Five Forces (~45 sec)

> Starting with the external environment. Two forces are critical: substitutes — digital platforms like JD Enterprise and 1688 are directly disintermediating traditional distributors; and rivalry — giants like TechData dominate scale while grey shops undercut on price. Buyer power is mid-to-high: procurement managers are cost-sensitive but will pay a premium for verified authenticity on high-value components. Supplier power is medium — Tier-1 contracts actually protect HiTech. And new entrants face a real barrier because Tier-1 credentials are genuinely hard to obtain.
>
> The strategic implication: position where forces are weakest — a narrow, trust-driven niche.

---

## SLIDE 12 — VRIO (~45 sec)

> Now the internal audit. HiTech's 8-year trust network scores V, R, and I — it's valuable, rare, and costly to imitate because of social complexity. But the O column — organized — is a clear no. That advantage is locked in Ben's personal relationships with zero digital expression. The Tier-1 contracts are similar: valuable and rare, but invisible online. And the manual PDF quoting process? It fails every VRIO test — it's a competitive disadvantage creating a 48-hour bottleneck.
>
> The fix is the O column. Organize what's already there.

---

## SLIDE 13 — Isolating Mechanisms (~30 sec)

> Why can't competitors just copy this? HiTech possesses all three isolating mechanisms from Class 3. Unique history — 8 years surviving two GPU boom-bust cycles. Causal ambiguity — even Ben himself cannot fully articulate why his clients stay loyal. And social complexity — the trust network spans customs agents, warehouse operators, and procurement leads in ways that resist systematic replication. All three present simultaneously — that's rare for any company, let alone an SME.

---

## SLIDE 14 — Value Chain (~30 sec)

> The value chain shows a distinctive pattern: strong at both ends, broken in the middle. Inbound logistics — excellent, powered by Tier-1 direct sourcing. Customer service — excellent, deep one-on-one relationships. But marketing and sales? A structural gap. Our strategy is surgical: weld the marketing midline only. Don't touch what already works.

---

## SLIDE 15 — Where We Compete (~35 sec)

> Class 4 demands a clear positioning choice. Broad cost leadership against giants like TechData? Suicide — they have 100x our scale. Narrow cost against grey shops? Also death — they'll always be cheaper. Broad differentiation? We lack the catalog breadth.
>
> The only viable quadrant is focused differentiation: serve a narrow segment of mid-market mainland buyers who need verified authenticity and fast cross-border response. This is exactly where HiTech's VRIO assets create defensible advantage.

---

## SLIDE 16 — Three Pillars (~30 sec)

> We execute through three tightly integrated pillars — modeled on Southwest Airlines' activity system from Class 4. Pillar 1: Presence on 1688 with long-tail SKU keywords. Pillar 2: Proof through Baidu SEO content and Tier-1 certificate trust proxies. Pillar 3: Velocity — compressing response time from 48 hours to 4 hours. Each pillar feeds the next. Imitators must copy all three at once. None can copy any one cheaply.

---

## SLIDE 17 — Pillar 1: 1688 Presence (~25 sec)

> Pillar 1 in detail. We open a ¥2,000-per-year 1688 storefront. No broad advertising — only long-tail keywords like "RTX 4090 HK Tier-1 authorized stock." We list 5 hero SKUs first: high-search, high-margin GPU and NVMe products. Target: go from zero to 5,000 targeted monthly impressions within 90 days.

---

## SLIDE 18 — Pillar 2: Trust Proxies (~25 sec)

> Pillar 2: content authority. We publish Baidu-indexed authenticity guides — practical articles like "how to spot fake GPUs" and "refurbished NVMe red flags." Product pages feature watermarked Tier-1 contract scans. When a buyer finds HiTech on 1688 and then searches on Baidu, they find expert content plus verifiable proof. Eighty percent of the selling happens before first contact. And the cost? Zero — authored in-house by our team.

---

## SLIDE 19 — Pillar 3: 4-Hour Velocity (~30 sec)

> Pillar 3: speed. The current 48-hour manual PDF workflow is a conversion killer. Our fix: route 1688 inquiries through a structured Google Form that triggers an instant WeChat alert to Ben's phone. Zero new hires, zero software cost. Average response drops to under 4 hours. In mainland B2B procurement, speed beats price. We project a 20% conversion lift from this single change.

---

## SLIDE 20 — Divider: Benefits (~10 sec)

> Step 4: Reinforce Key Benefits. Let's tie everything back to Ben's original problem with hard numbers.

---

## SLIDE 21 — Budget (~30 sec)

> The budget is locked at ¥5,000 maximum. The 1688 storefront costs ¥2,000 — that's the only fixed commitment. The remaining ¥3,000 is a flexible buffer released only after Phase B validates the model. Content production and form routing cost nothing — internal labor and free cloud tools. Total external risk exposure: ¥2,000 to ¥5,000. Ultra-light, reversible, family-safe.

---

## SLIDE 22 — 12-Month Returns (~25 sec)

> Three target outcomes at 12 months. Search visibility: 25x increase, from under 200 to over 5,000 impressions per month. New accounts: 10 or more, breaking the 40-account ceiling. Revenue growth: plus 15 to 20 percent, at least HK$500,000 incremental. That's ¥5,000 in, HK$500,000 out — a 100-to-1 ROI scale.

---

## SLIDE 23 — Roadmap (~25 sec)

> The roadmap has four phases. Months 1-2: foundation — open 1688, publish three SEO articles. Months 3-4: velocity — form goes live, WeChat alerts active, 4-hour SLA operational. Months 5-6: tune — release the ¥3,000 buffer on proven top SKUs. Months 7-12: scale to 10-plus accounts and activate the referral loop. Each phase has a kill-switch. We validate before we spend.

---

## SLIDE 24 — Have We Tested It? (Q7) (~30 sec)

> Have we just proposed this, or actually tested it? Three pilots. First, we drafted a live 1688 SKU page and showed it to 5 mainland buyers — 4 out of 5 said they would send an inquiry. Second, we wired the Google Form to WeChat alert pipeline in one day — average response time in testing was 3 hours 12 minutes. Third, we published one SEO article that Baidu indexed within 9 days, generating an organic inquiry within two weeks. Each pillar has been prototyped, not just proposed.

---

## SLIDE 25 — Risks & Defences (~25 sec)

> Three risks, each with a pre-staged defence. Imitation: grey shops copy our keywords — but they cannot fake the Tier-1 certification wall. Devaluation: some buyers chase the cheapest grey goods — our content includes real burn-board horror stories that make the risk tangible. Compliance: cross-border data and customs rules may shift — we use only licensed couriers and collect zero personal data on our forms.

## SLIDE 29 — Divider: Behind the Scenes (~10 sec)

> Thank you. Before Q&A, we'd like to spend about five minutes on what happened behind the scenes — the real difficulties, how we addressed them, and what we honestly still can't solve.

---

## SLIDE 30 — Difficulties We Hit (~1.5 min)

> Three major difficulties. First, restricted data access. HiTech stores everything in Excel spreadsheets, but Ben rationed what he shared with us. Why? Because mid-sized resellers genuinely fear copy-stocking — grey shops cloning their catalog and product mix to undercut them. So we had to analyse a business through a half-open window.
>
> Second, internal disagreement. Our group split on marketing direction. Some members pushed hard for TikTok and RedNote influencer campaigns — that's the trendy playbook right now. Others argued for boring B2B search optimization. We lost two weeks to circular debate before forcing a data-driven tiebreaker.
>
> Third, coordination friction. Five members, four concurrent internships, two cities. Our available sync window was under three hours per week. Slides drafted independently kept drifting in tone and logic — the classic patchwork problem.

---

## SLIDE 31 — How We Addressed Them (~1.5 min)

> Each fix maps directly to one difficulty. For restricted data access, we signed a mutual NDA, anonymised all SKUs in our deck, and let Ben redact sensitive margins before we reviewed them. That earned enough trust to unlock the Excel data we needed for VRIO and Value Chain analysis.
>
> For the TikTok debate, we stopped arguing opinions and ran a real audit. We interviewed 10 actual mainland procurement managers about their sourcing workflow. The result was brutal: zero out of ten use TikTok or RedNote to find GPU suppliers. B2B purchasing starts with intentional platform search. Debate ended in one meeting — and we saved 100% of our marketing budget from being wasted on the wrong channel.
>
> For coordination, we borrowed from value chain coordination theory. One member owned the logic spine of the entire deck. The other four rotated as "grading professor" every Saturday, attacking weak slides with the same criteria our real professor uses. This red-team rhythm kept the tone consistent end-to-end.

---

## SLIDE 32 — What We Still Can't Solve (~1.5 min)

> We want to be transparent about three open questions we're bringing to the class.
>
> First, the Verification Wall. 1688 Gold Member status typically requires a mainland business license and an onshore RMB bank account. HiTech is a pure Hong Kong SME. Setting up a mainland subsidiary would destroy the lean-cost advantage that's core to their VRIO profile. Can an HK-only firm get verified without that cost? We'd love to hear if anyone has seen a compliant workaround.
>
> Second, the Content Defence dilemma. Our strategy converts Ben's tacit knowledge — how to spot fake hardware — into public digital content. But once it's published, AI scrapers can clone it overnight. What keeps the trust premium alive after the text itself is copied? We believe the Tier-1 certification is the non-copyable anchor, but we're not fully certain how long that holds.
>
> Third, measuring trust itself. We can track impressions, inquiries, and conversion rates. But we cannot directly measure whether buyers actually trust HiTech more than grey shops. What proxy metric would the class recommend for trust? Repeat order rate? Time-to-first-order? We're genuinely asking.
>
> These aren't gaps in our deck — they're live research questions. We bring them to you and to the professor for collective input.
